About

I am Derek Okafor. I grew up in Northern Virginia and never really left. Ashburn is home, has been for most of my life. The whole Loudoun County stretch from Herndon to Sterling to Ashburn is basically one long corridor of data centers and strip malls and I have worked in most of the data centers at this point.

I studied network engineering at Northern Virginia Community College, got my AAS in information systems technology in 2014. Right out of school I took a NOC technician job at a regional ISP in Herndon. That was five years of monitoring circuits, chasing fiber cuts, and learning how to stay awake on overnight shifts. After that I moved to a colocation facility in Sterling where I did hands-on data center work for about six years. Racking servers, running cable, managing power and cooling, dealing with customer cages. Learned a lot about how things actually break when you are the one standing in front of the rack at 3am.

When the GPU wave hit and companies started building out training clusters, I caught on with a staffing firm that placed me as a contract cluster reliability engineer. That is what I do now. I keep distributed GPU training infrastructure running. When a node drops out of a multi-day training job at 2am, I am the one who gets paged. I know the cluster topology, the fabric, the orchestration layer, the failure patterns. I work with Slurm, Kubernetes, InfiniBand, DCGM, Prometheus, Grafana and a lot of custom tooling that holds everything together.

At home I have four used A6000s in a closet that I use as a homelab. They pull more power than my landlord probably expects and they make the closet uncomfortably warm, but they let me test tooling and reproduce failure modes without touching production. Tinkering time is limited though. My wife is a nurse and we have a two year old, so most of my free time is not actually free. I get maybe an hour after bedtime if I am not on call.

My interests are mostly around distributed systems, GPU cluster operations, network engineering, data center infrastructure, failure analysis and practical monitoring tooling. I am not a researcher or a model developer. I keep the hardware and the orchestration running so that the people who do that work can keep going.
