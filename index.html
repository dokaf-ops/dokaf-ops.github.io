Field Note: Three Ways a Training Cluster Lies Before a Node Drops

Large GPU training clusters rarely fail cleanly. A node that is about to drop usually lies first, reporting nominal health while subtle indicators accumulate. Here are three patterns I see repeatedly.

1. The Healthy-But-Slow Heartbeat

Node health checks return OK, but heartbeat latency creeps from the usual 2 to 4 ms band up toward 15 ms over several minutes. Schedulers do not flag this because the check is binary: alive or not. The lag is often the first sign of a degrading NIC or a PCIe link retraining silently.

2. NCCL Allreduce Succeeds: At a Penalty

With NCCL_DEBUG=INFO, allreduce calls still complete, but the per-rail bandwidth drops asymmetrically. One rail might show 80 GB/s while a sibling drops to 40 GB/s, suggesting a single NIC or switch ASIC is struggling. The job keeps running; collective time just doubles.

3. NVML Says Fine, DCGM Says Otherwise

nvidia-smi (backed by NVML) may report GPU clocks and temperatures within spec, while DCGM health diagnostics flag an emerging row-remapping event or an uncorrectable ECC count climbing. The two views diverge before the hard fault.

The pattern: trust the gradient of the signal, not the binary gate. Heartbeat creep, asymmetric rail bandwidth, and NVML/DCGM divergence together form a reliable early-warning triangle.
